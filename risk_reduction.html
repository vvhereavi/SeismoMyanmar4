Evaluation Methodology & Purpose

Plan for People

1. Before an Earthquake: Preparation & Kits Home Safety Checklist Anchor Heavy Items: Secure bookcases, heavy furniture, TVs, and wall decor to wall studs. Latch Cabinets: Install latches on kitchen and storage cabinets to keep contents from falling out. Know Your Utilities: Learn how and where to shut off your main gas and water valves to prevent fires and flooding.

2. During an Earthquake: Core Actions The "Drop, Cover, and Hold On" Drill DROP: Drop onto your hands and knees immediately. This keeps you from being knocked off balance and allows you to crawl. COVER: Cover your head and neck with your arms. Crawl under a sturdy table or desk if available. If no shelter is nearby, crawl to an interior wall away from windows, staying bent low to protect your vital organs. HOLD ON: Hold tightly to your shelter with one hand until the shaking stops. Use your other arm to protect your head and neck. Specialized Scenarios If in Bed: Stay in bed. Cover your head and neck with a pillow until shaking stops. If Outdoors: Drop to the ground and crawl toward an open area. Stay clear of buildings, power lines, streetlights, and trees. If Driving: Pull over safely in a clear spot away from overpasses, power lines, and structures. Set the parking brake and remain inside your vehicle until shaking stops. Proceed with caution afterward, avoiding damaged bridges or ramps. If Using a Wheelchair or Walker: Lock your wheels immediately. Stay seated, bend over, and cover your head and neck with your arms, a pillow, or a book.

3. After an Earthquake: Immediate Response & Safety First Actions Pause Before Moving: Wait at least one minute after shaking stops to inspect your immediate surroundings for falling hazards. Put On Shoes: Put on sturdy shoes right away to prevent foot injuries from broken glass and debris. Check Environment: Use flashlights—never candles, matches, or lighters—to check for damage. Avoid using electrical light switches until you are certain there are no natural gas leaks (sparks can trigger an explosion). Evacuate Safely: If your building is structurally damaged, grab your Go-Kit and move to an open area outside. If no safe open space exists nearby, remain indoors. Aftershocks & Tsunami Risk Expect Aftershocks: Be ready to Drop, Cover, and Hold On again whenever secondary shaking occurs. Tsunami Hazard (Coastal Areas): If you are near the coast, do not wait for an official warning. As soon as shaking stops, immediately walk quickly inland or to high ground. If Trapped Under Debris Protect Airways: Cover your nose and mouth with a dust mask, cloth, or piece of clothing to avoid inhaling airborne dust and concrete particulate. Signal Rescuers: Blow a whistle or knock on a solid structural element three times every few minutes. Save your voice to prevent exhaustion and dust inhalation.

Risk Reduction Plan for Buildings

Plan for Buildings

People can drop, cover and hold on — buildings cannot. The measures below protect the structure itself and reduce collapse and non-structural damage before, during and after an earthquake.

1. Before an Earthquake — Build & Strengthen

  • Follow the building code: have designs checked and construction supervised by qualified engineers, especially for schools, hospitals and multi-storey buildings.
  • Retrofit weak structures: strengthen unreinforced masonry and soft ground storeys with shear walls, cross-bracing or column jacketing.
  • Anchor non-structural parts: bolt down parapets, roof slabs, chimneys, water tanks, signboards and heavy cladding so they cannot fall on people below.
  • Protect utilities: use flexible gas and water connections and install an automatic gas shut-off valve to prevent post-earthquake fires.
  • Choose and prepare the site: avoid building across active fault traces, on steep slopes or on soft reclaimed soil, and keep clear evacuation space around the building.

2. During an Earthquake — Built-in Safeguards

  • Elevators automatically return to the ground floor and lock out — use stairs only after the building has been inspected.
  • Automatic gas shut-off valves isolate the fuel supply; emergency lighting and fire alarms switch to backup power.
  • Occupants shelter away from glazed facades, suspended ceilings and heavy rooftop equipment.

3. After an Earthquake — Inspect Before Re-entry

  • Do not re-enter until a qualified engineer completes a rapid visual assessment and marks the building safe or unsafe.
  • Check load-bearing walls, columns, beams, staircases and utility connections for cracks, deformation or leaks.
  • Cordon off falling hazards such as cracked parapets, facade tiles and damaged water tanks, and report suspected structural damage to the township authorities.
  • Expect aftershocks: shore up damaged elements and keep exits and evacuation routes clear.
